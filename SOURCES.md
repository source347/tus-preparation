# Sources and evidence standards

The repository may use information gathered from public medical and educational sources across the web. Every submitted card must identify the sources that support it.

## Preferred source hierarchy

1. Current clinical guidelines and consensus statements from recognized professional societies.
2. Standard medical textbooks and established reference works.
3. Peer-reviewed review articles and primary research when relevant.
4. Reputable university, hospital, and public-health educational resources.

Use secondary summaries cautiously, especially for treatment recommendations, diagnostic criteria, and rapidly changing topics.

## Source recording

For each card, provide the source title, direct URL, and access date. Where possible, cite the relevant chapter, section, table, or guideline version.

## Verification

- Confirm important facts against a primary or authoritative source.
- Check publication date and whether recommendations have been superseded.
- Distinguish established facts from emerging or contested evidence.
- For clinical recommendations, record the guideline organization and version/year.
- Do not treat search snippets, social-media posts, or unsourced study notes as sufficient evidence.

## Copyright and licensing

Publicly accessible does not mean public domain. Do not reproduce textbook passages, proprietary flashcards, paid-course materials, or protected exam questions and explanations. Summarize facts in original wording and link to sources. Respect each source's license and terms of use.

## Medical disclaimer

This repository is an educational study aid, not clinical guidance. Contributors and learners should verify current recommendations using authoritative sources.
